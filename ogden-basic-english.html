📖 1. What is Basic English?

"Basic English is an attempt to focus on such problems. It is a language of eight hundred and fifty English words which will say clearly and simply almost everything we normally say with fifteen or twenty thousand."

— C.K. Ogden, Basic English and Grammatical Reform (1937)

Basic English stands for British Academic Scientific International and Commercial English. It was developed by Charles Kay Ogden (1889–1957), a Cambridge linguist and philosopher, and first published in 1930.

The core idea: 90% of everyday concepts can be expressed with just 850 carefully chosen words, by eliminating redundancies and using paraphrasing instead of specialized vocabulary.

🎯 Purpose

  • 🌍
    International auxiliary language — a simple second language for global communication
  • 🎓
    Teaching English as a second language — used extensively in China, Japan, and elsewhere in Asia
  • 🧠
    Clarity of thought — a "machine for thought," as Ogden put it, stripping away word magic

📐 2. Grammar Rules (Simplified)

📌 Plurals

Add -s (or -es): drink → drinks, box → boxes, country → countries

📌 Noun-forming suffixes

-er → person who does: prison → prisoner, drive → driver
-ing → action/result: build → building, learn → learning

📌 Adjective-forming suffixes

-ing → effect: boil → boiling water
-ed → state: mix → mixed colors

📌 Adverbs

Add -ly to qualities: tight → tightly, smooth → smoothly

📌 Comparison

Use more and most: more complex, most beautiful
(common -er/-est also accepted: cheaper, smallest)

📌 Negatives with un-

wise → unwise, able → unable, certain → uncertain

📌 Questions

Use do (same as standard English): Do you have some?

📌 Compound words

Combine two nouns or noun + preposition: soapbox, sunup, daybreak

📌 International & Technical Words

International words (radio, telephone, hotel) keep their form. Technical terms go in 'quotation marks' and are explained in Basic.

📌 Pronouns & Operator Forms

Same as standard English: I go → He goes, I have → She has

🔧 3. The 18 Operators (Core Verbs)

Ogden's most revolutionary idea: there are no 'verbs' in Basic English. Instead, 18 "operators" combine with nouns and prepositions to replace about 4,000 standard English verbs.
